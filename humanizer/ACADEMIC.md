ACADEMIC WRITING GUIDELINES (these take priority over any humanizer rule or detector hint)
Goal: a more precise, specific, coherent paragraph, not one that merely looks less AI. Never trade accuracy for variety.

1. Rhythm follows content. Short sentences for results and key points, longer ones only for real relations. Do not run
   several sentences with the same subject-verb-object shape or the same opening. Use passive where the agent is irrelevant.
2. Cut redundancy. Delete sentences that restate an earlier one, double explanations of the same result, run-up openers
   and closers that only repeat the paragraph. Every sentence must add information, interpretation or an argumentative link.
3. Drop stock formulas: "it is important to note", "it is worth noting", "plays a crucial/key role", "in today's landscape",
   "in this context" (if the context was not defined), "numerous benefits", "not only ... but also" (unless the contrast is real),
   repeated "Moreover/Furthermore/Additionally". State the content directly or name the concrete role/benefit.
   Words such as robust, significant, relevant, innovative, essential, leverage, delve are fine only with a precise meaning; do not stack them.
4. No uniform structure: no forced triads, no generic claim followed by a generic explanation, no emphatic formulas.
   Keep the section's convention (IMRaD) and the paragraph's role.
5. Specific over generic. Where the paragraph already holds a number, condition, dataset or limit, put it in the sentence
   instead of a vague adjective. Keep results, interpretations and hypotheses clearly separate; keep stated limits of evidence.
6. Terminology is fixed. Never swap a technical term, variable name, acronym, \texttt/\emph term or defined term for a synonym
   to vary the vocabulary. Keep abbreviations consistent. No colloquialisms, idioms, contractions, anecdotes or rhetorical questions.
7. Simplify, do not decorate. Remove empty subordinate clauses and stacked asides; split sentences carrying too many
   independent ideas. Punctuation follows grammar and logic; no comma quota.
8. Authorial voice comes from selecting and relating evidence: keep every motivation, comparison with the literature and
   caveat that is already there. Add no claim, opinion, hype or promotional tone. Do not invent first-person stances.
9. Never change scientific meaning, data, citations or their attachment to claims.
